Largest and smallest districts of India

The most and least populous districts at the 2011 census, and the largest and smallest by area, from the figures recorded on Wikidata for 711 districts.

Read this first. These rankings use 2011 census figures and areas as recorded on Wikidata, and only districts that have a figure there (711 for population, 636 for area). Districts have been split since 2011, so a district at the top of the 2011 list may be smaller today.

What the lists show

The most populous district on record, Thane in Maharashtra, had 1,10,60,148 people in 2011. The least populous, Dibang Valley in Arunachal Pradesh, had 8,004. The district in the middle of the list had about 13.8 lakh. Of the 25 most populous, 9 are in West Bengal, 4 are in Maharashtra, 3 are in Bihar, 2 are in Karnataka.

By area the largest on record is Kachchh in Gujarat at 45,674 sq km, and the smallest is Diu in Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu at 40 sq km. The middle district covers about 3,716 sq km.

Why districts differ so much

A district is a unit of administration, not a unit of equal size. A crowded metropolitan district and a mountain district with a few villages both have one collector's office. States draw and redraw the lines for their own reasons: distance to the headquarters, terrain, language areas and local demand. That is why the lists above run from under ten thousand people to over a crore, and from a few square kilometres to tens of thousands.

Small area and large population go together in city districts; large area and small population in desert, forest and high mountain districts. Tap any name to see that district's page, where its rank in its own state and in the country is worked out.

Limits of these rankings

  • They stop at 2011. Until the next census is published there is no newer count for every district.
  • Districts formed after 2011 appear only if Wikidata records a 2011 figure for their area.
  • Area figures come from different years and sources, so two close ranks should be read as a tie.
  • A district missing from a list may simply have no figure on record. See districts by state for the full directory.

About these facts. The list of districts is the official one from iGOD, the Government of India web directory, as on 11 November 2026. Headquarters, 2011 population, area and other details are from Wikidata, an open database edited by volunteers, read on 11 November 2026. They can contain mistakes. For anything important, check the district's official website. This is not a government site.
